# Evaluation goal

Select timely, credible changes in mainland China's housing policy and key real estate indicators that materially improve the reader's understanding of the market.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Major shift.** A consequential nationwide or major-city policy takes effect, or a verified housing indicator shows an exceptional change with clear geographic and historical context.
- **7-8: Important.** A substantive national or local policy is formally issued or implemented; an authoritative indicator changes meaningfully against a comparable period or a clearly documented baseline; or a significant policy proposal has a specific issuer, text, and next step.
- **5-6: Useful.** A credible routine data release or narrower local adjustment adds context but shows limited change, or a proposal remains preliminary.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Repeated coverage, ordinary fluctuations without a meaningful comparison, commentary that adds no primary evidence, or a headline whose apparent movement is explained by a tiny base.
- **0-2: Noise.** Unsupported policy rumors, promotional listings, speculative price predictions, or numbers without identifiable source, geography, period, or unit.

# Evaluation guidance

Judge source authority, date, policy status, geographic reach, affected groups, and whether the article states what changed relative to an applicable baseline. Distinguish national from local measures and announced proposals from rules already in force. For data, preserve units, period, month-on-month versus year-on-year comparisons, and revisions when given. Do not infer an anomaly from a percentage alone; consider the base and comparability. A dramatic headline without enough evidence should not reach the publishing threshold.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
